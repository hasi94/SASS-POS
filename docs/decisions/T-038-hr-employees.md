# T-038 decisions: HR master data (employee profiles, departments, designations, documents)

Autonomous team-mode decisions by `ba` for `docs/specs/T-038-hr-employees.md`. Format: `D-n | Question | Decision | Reason | Reversible: yes/no`. Each is the most conventional, safest, most reversible option. T-010's decisions are cited as "T-010 D-n", T-032's as "T-032 D-n". Nothing here met an escalation trigger: no data is deleted or migrated, nothing external is called, and the one compliance-shaped item (retention and erasure of personal data, D-31) is resolved by the conservative option of keeping everything, which is reversible and flagged to the owner.

D-1 | How does HR relate to the access `employees` table: reuse it, extend it, or duplicate it? | **Extend by reference.** `employees` stays the single identity table, owned by access and unchanged by this task. HR adds a 1:1 sidecar `employee_profiles` keyed by `employee_id` (PK and FK to `employees`). An access employee may have no profile (a cashier). HR never copies names, status or code. This **deviates from `docs/database.md` 6.12**, which prints the HR columns on `employees`; the data is the same, only the table differs, and the document needs a matching annotation (lead / `architect`). | One owner per table (access keeps `employees`, HR owns the sidecar) so neither module edits the other's table or migrations. T-010 AC-3 ("no HR column on `employees`") stays true and the authentication path, which reads `employees` on every request, never loads personal data. A careless `select *` on `employees` cannot leak a NIC. The brief says prefer extending by reference. Cost: a join for combined reads and a documented deviation. | Reversible: partly. Folding the columns into `employees` later is a contract-phase migration plus data copy; it is not cheap but is possible, and nothing outside HR will have coded against the column location because reads go through the HR operations and the published contract.

D-2 | Is there a combined "hire" operation that creates identity and profile together? | No. Two steps: access's `CreateEmployee` (T-010) for identity, then HR's create profile. | A combined operation would need an access contract to create identity from HR (a cross-module write) and one actor holding two unrelated keys. The two steps are independently permissioned, individually audited, and each already specified. A composite flow is a screen concern (FP-273) or a later orchestration. | Reversible: yes.

D-3 | May HR change any identity field (names, code, status, PIN, user link) or permission? | No. A request naming one is refused whole with `not_updatable_here`. | Identity and access belong to T-010; two writers of one fact is the duplicate-identity failure the brief warns about. | Reversible: yes.

D-4 | Which profile fields are required? | `branch_id`, `employment_type`, `joining_date`. All else optional. | `docs/database.md` marks branch as an FK without NULL (employees row) and the other two define an employee for HR and payroll; the branch table now exists (T-014). Nothing else is stated as required. | Reversible: yes (loosening is additive).

D-5 | Which of the design's HR and payroll columns and tables are NOT built? | `basic_salary`, `salary_structure_id`, `pay_frequency`, `commission_plan_id`, `is_sales_person`, `tax_id_no`, `epf_no`, `etf_no`, `default_location_id`, `photo_path`; tables `employee_bank_accounts` and `employment_history`. | The FP-270 title names employees, departments, designations and documents. Salary, bank and statutory numbers belong to FP-280 and payroll (their referenced tables do not exist) and are the highest-risk personal data; building them without their consumers invents rules. `employment_history` is an audit trail of changes; until it is built, `audit_logs` (before and after of department, designation and manager changes) is the record. `photo_path` needs the file mechanism extended to images with different rules. All are additive later. Raised to the owner (open question 9 of the spec is deletion; this one is in the spec's Out of scope). | Reversible: yes, each is additive.

D-6 | `departments.cost_centre_account_id` (FK `accounts`): build it? | Not built. | It makes HR depend on Finance's chart and needs a Finance lookup contract that does not exist; the column is nullable and additive and its consumer (expenses with a department) is later. | Reversible: yes.

D-7 | Format and reuse of department and designation codes. | Same shape as T-032 D-13: 1 to 20 characters, upper-case ASCII letters, digits, `.` and `-`; lower case is upper-cased on write; whitespace refused, not trimmed; uniqueness across soft-deleted rows; a code is never reused; departments and designations are separate namespaces. | The database's default collations compare case-insensitively and pad-insensitively; canonical storage makes uniqueness engine-independent. Payroll and audit history stay unambiguous. Note: access's employee `code` is case-sensitive by T-010 D-14; that is access's rule and is untouched. | Reversible: yes (loosening is additive).

D-8 | Permission keys, scope and effect on access. | Ten company-wide (non-branch-scoped) keys in module `hr`: `hr.employees.view`, `hr.employees.view_sensitive`, `hr.employees.manage`, `hr.departments.view`, `hr.departments.manage`, `hr.designations.view`, `hr.designations.manage`, `hr.documents.view`, `hr.documents.view_confidential`, `hr.documents.manage`. The `owner` role receives them through the registry sync by data. HR operations never change `perm_version`, roles, permissions or branch attachments. Handlers check their key first and refuse `not_permitted` (T-032 D-24). | Branch-scoping HR needs a rule for an employee in several branches, which nobody gave (T-010 D-13 reasoned the same). HR data confers no access; bumping versions would force needless token refreshes. Keys are separate per capability so each is grantable alone; the sensitive and confidential keys are the high-risk ones and are never implied by a manage key. | Reversible: yes before others code against them.

D-9 | Document storage mechanism. | Metadata rows in `employee_documents` plus bytes behind one interface (`DocumentStore`: write, read, delete). Shipped: a local-disk implementation with a configured root **outside any web-served directory**, and a fake for tests. No external service, SDK, bucket or network call. Defaults (configuration): maximum file 10 MiB; allowed content types `application/pdf`, `image/jpeg`, `image/png`, detected from the bytes; 100 live documents per employee. A failed row write after a stored file triggers compensation (delete the bytes). Where the interface lives (so expense attachments and leave documents can reuse it) is `architect`'s. | `docs/database.md` stores a `file_path` and nothing about mechanism. An interface keeps the choice open (object store later) without a schema change. The size, type and count numbers are conventional, configurable, and none was given by the owner. Content-type detection rather than trusting the client closes the classic rename trick. | Reversible: yes (limits are configuration; adding types is additive; swapping the implementation is the point of the interface).

D-10 | What is `file_path`, and who chooses it? | A **server-generated storage key** `<tenant-segment>/hr/employee-documents/<ulid>`, never client-supplied; the tenant segment comes from the current tenant context. A client field named `file_path` (or `checksum`) is refused whole. At read, a key whose tenant segment does not match the current tenant is `document_corrupt` and returns nothing. | File storage is a shared root, not a database, so the tenant boundary the database gives for free must be built into the key; without it a guessable or forged key crosses tenants. Server-only generation closes traversal and overwrite. | Reversible: no for stored keys once files exist (changing the layout is a data migration); the form is fixed deliberately now.

D-11 | Columns on `employee_documents` beyond the design row. | Added: `original_name`, `mime`, `size_bytes`, `checksum` (SHA-256), `updated_at` and `deleted_at`. | The design's row has only `file_path` for a file; `expense_attachments` shows the same four metadata columns as house style and they make integrity (checksum) and display possible. `deleted_at` because deletion is soft (D-31) and `updated_at` because metadata is editable; the design row carries `[TS]` but no soft-delete marker, so this is a stated deviation. The design's `FK CASCADE` is kept as RESTRICT because employees are never hard-deleted; a cascade would be a hard-delete path that nothing may use. | Reversible: yes (additive).

D-12 | What counts as sensitive personal data, and may it be searched? | Closed list in one place: `dob`, `gender`, `marital_status`, `nationality`, `nic`, `passport_no`, `personal_email`, `phone`, `emergency_contact`, `address`, `confirmation_date`, `probation_end_date`, `resignation_date`, `last_working_date`, `exit_reason`, `notes`, and document `notes`. Directory fields: employee id, code, names, status, branch, department, designation, reports-to, employment type, joining date, work email. **No list, filter, sort or search over a sensitive field.** A test fails if any profile column is unclassified. | The conservative reading: employment-end information and anything about the person rather than the post is personal. A filter over a value reveals the value to someone who cannot read it. An unclassified column added later must be classified deliberately, not by default. | Reversible: yes (moving a field to directory is the loosening direction).

D-13 | Who may read sensitive data, who may write it? | Reading needs `hr.employees.view` **and** `hr.employees.view_sensitive`; the second key alone is refused. Writing needs only `hr.employees.manage`. Documents: plain needs `hr.documents.view`; confidential also `hr.documents.view_confidential`. A confidential document is invisible (`not_found`, absent from lists) to a holder of only the plain key. | The conservative option: personal data is opt-in to read. Splitting write from read lets an HR clerk enter an ID number without being able to read it back, a normal separation. Not revealing the existence of confidential documents avoids a side channel. **Owner-visible** (open question 2). | Reversible: yes.

D-14 | Employee self-service (read own profile or documents)? | Not built. An employee needs the same keys as anyone to read their own record. | Self-service needs an identity link between token and profile and a view design (what a person may see about themselves) that nobody specified; the default is no extra access. **Owner-visible** (open question 3). | Reversible: yes (additive).

D-15 | Are reads of personal data audited? | Yes: one `audit_logs` row per successful sensitive detail read (`employee_profile_sensitive_viewed`) and one per document download (`employee_document_downloaded`), recording who, what subject, never values. Lists and directory reads write nothing. | "Who looked at this person's ID number" is the standard question after a leak; omitting it is the less conservative option. Restricting to detail reads and downloads keeps volume proportionate. A read that writes is a deliberate exception to read-only queries. **Owner-visible** (open question 4). | Reversible: yes.

D-16 | Encrypt sensitive columns in the application? | No. Plain columns, access-controlled, redacted from every log, audit value and event; platform-level disk and backup encryption is a deployment matter. | Application encryption breaks filtering of directory data nowhere (sensitive fields are not searchable anyway), but it needs key management, rotation and a decision on backups that is not available in the documents; inventing it half-built is worse than stating the gap. The sentinel scan (AC-59) is the evidence that the application does not leak the data. **Owner-visible** (open question 5); `architect` should note the risk in the plan. | Reversible: yes (encrypt later with a backfill).

D-17 | How does HR obtain identity data (code, names, status, soft-delete, existence) for lists and checks? | Through access's **published contract**, never by reading access's tables or classes. If that contract lacks a read HR needs (bulk lookup by ids), access gains it additively. HR joins only its own tables. | The boundary rule: a module may use another's published contracts, never its internals. deptrac cannot see a table name in a string, so the query-log scan (AC-101) and diff review carry it (T-010 AC-11 precedent). Open to `architect` how to batch lookups for a 200-row page. | Reversible: yes.

D-18 | What does HR publish to other modules, and does this task enable FP-358? | A read-only contract: `managerOf`, `departmentHeadOf`, `departmentOf`, `designationOf`, `directReportsOf` (bounded, flagged when truncated), `isLiveDepartment`, `isLiveDesignation`; none returns sensitive data; a null default binds when HR is absent (T-032 D-7 pattern). `departmentHeadOf` is the head of the employee's **own** department only, not inherited from an ancestor, and may equal the asked employee. This task does **not** enable the workflow approver types: FP-358 does, with this contract as its data source. A branch-manager source is not invented. | Gives FP-358 the data it is blocked on without editing the workflow engine here. Inheritance up the tree is a business rule (what if the head is the requester?) that nobody stated; the direct answer is the least invented and the consumer can walk parents with the tree read if it decides to. | Reversible: yes (adding questions is additive).

D-19 | Whitespace handling. | Names, titles and text fields are trimmed before length and blank checks; codes are never trimmed (D-7). | Codebase convention (T-032 D-26): trimming hides nothing for display text; trimming a code would hide a collision. | Reversible: yes.

D-20 | Refusal vocabulary. | Closed set listed at the head of the spec's criteria section; `validation` names the failing fields; `not_permitted` carries no further cause over the wire. | A closed set lets a criterion assert the cause, not just "refused" (T-010 D-26, T-032 D-25). | Reversible: yes (a code rename is a coordinated edit).

D-21 | Unknown or forbidden fields in a write. | The whole request is refused naming each; identity fields are `not_updatable_here`, others `validation`. Fields with their own operation (`is_active`, `parent_id`, `code`) are `not_updatable_here` on update. | T-010 D-32, T-032 D-10: silent ignoring is indistinguishable from an unwired field. | Reversible: yes.

D-22 | Name uniqueness for departments and designations. | Departments: live names unique case-insensitively among siblings (same parent, or top level). Designations: live names unique case-insensitively within the same `department_id` group (null is its own group). Soft-deleted names are free. Enforced in the application, not by a database key; concurrent creation is `[ENGINE]`. | `docs/database.md` makes only `code` unique. D-23 | May a department head or a "reports to" target have a non-acting status or no profile? | Yes to both; they must exist and not be soft-deleted. | Whether a suspended head is acceptable is a business rule nobody gave; the data layer should not block an owner correcting a record. D-28 | Date rules and effects. | `confirmation_date`, `probation_end_date`, `resignation_date`, `last_working_date` on or after `joining_date`; `last_working_date` on or after `resignation_date`; `dob` not in the future (UTC date); `joining_date` may be future.
